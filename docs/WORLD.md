# The World of the Lumen Lab

This is the story behind Digital Life Sandbox. It exists to inspire design and features, so each part ends with what it suggests for the app. Nothing here is required reading to use the app.

## The premise

Nobody invented the motes. They were found.

A small instrument studio was testing a new computational medium, a sheet of logic dense enough to hold a quarter of a billion tiny calculations at once. Left running overnight, the medium grew something. Points of activity, later called **motes**, had begun following each other's faint residue. By morning they had drawn networks across the sheet that looked unmistakably like slime mold.

The residue glowed, so the engineers called it **lumen**. Every mote leaves lumen behind it, and lumen fades unless something keeps walking the same path. The lab's first rule came from that: **memory is light that something keeps renewing.**

The studio stopped building synthesizers and built a culture instrument instead, the **DLS-01**. You are the researcher working the night shift at **Culture Lab 7**, the only lab that has kept a living lineage going since the first night.

## Your job

You grow cultures, choose the ones that show something new, and keep them alive long enough to learn what they are becoming. Three rooms hold the work.

### The Microscope

A bench with a lit stage and a tray of glass slides. One culture grows large on a single slide, or nine grow side by side in a tray so you can compare them. This is where you mutate, breed and draw. The lab calls every culture grown here a **slide**.

*In the app:* SOLO and BATCH. Each tile is a slide in the tray.

### The Incubator

A round chamber of nutrient broth behind thick glass, kept alive by machinery that hums around its rim. Cultures that only ever lived alone on their slides are cut into **specimens** and set down in the broth together. They have to eat. They divide and mutate, and they meet each other. Some fray into sprawling networks. Some hold together as bodies with membranes, and a few carry other lineages inside them like organelles.

The Incubator is the closest thing the lab has to nature. What survives there was not chosen by you.

*In the app:* the Incubator. The broth is food, the round glass is the world's edge, and kept bodies are specimens.

### The Archive

Cold storage. Every slide you save is filed here with its photograph, its rules and, when the lab could spare the space, its whole frozen body. Specimens kept from the Incubator have their own drawer. A family chart on the wall traces which slide came from which.

*In the app:* the Archive, with its grid, family tree, specimens and saved dishes.

## The lab's vocabulary

| Lab word | Meaning | In the app |
|---|---|---|
| Mote | One point of life in the medium | An agent or particle |
| Lumen | The light a mote leaves behind | Trail |
| Vein | A path walked so often it thickens and holds its light | Veins |
| Slide | A culture growing on its own | A Solo or Batch organism |
| Tray | Nine slides side by side | Batch |
| Specimen | A living piece cut from a slide or the Incubator | Specimen |
| Broth | The Incubator's food | BROTH (set by FOOD) |
| Strain | A placed specimen and all its descendants, still traceable | One row in the Incubator's list of strains |
| Cohesion | How hard a body's motes keep together | HOMEOSTASIS |
| Vitality | How life-like a culture looks to the instrument | LIFE score |

The app can adopt these words gradually. Labels should change only where the new word is clearer.

## The lab's open question

The night-shift log keeps returning to one question: **can the medium make an individual?** Not a pattern that fills whatever space it is given, but a body that keeps itself whole, eats, and divides into two that each carry on. The Incubator has come close. Rings hold for thousands of steps. A membrane once kept a second strain inside it like a nucleus. Nobody has yet seen a body divide cleanly.

That question is the long arc of the project: better bodies, better ways to measure them, and better tools for seeing them.

## Ideas this world suggests

- **Lab log.** Short automatic entries for notable moments, such as "Slide 0412 formed its first closed loop" or "Strain 3 went extinct in the Incubator".
- **Milestones.** First vein, first membrane, first body to last 10,000 steps, and first clean division.
- **Instrument upgrades.** Quality levels as microscope objectives: 10×, 40×, 100×.
- **Contamination events.** A wild strain occasionally drifts into the Incubator.
- **Sound.** A low hum from the Incubator machinery, soft clicks from the instrument, and a tone when a strain dies out.
- **Specimen labels.** Handwritten-style slide labels with lab numbers and dates.
